THE METHOD OF SHARED CONCERN · THE BELAY LINE
Pikas & Rigby — for covert group bullying & exclusion. Sidesteps accusation and defensiveness.
1
Low-Stakes 1-on-1 Exploratory Interviews
5–7 min · private · every bystander + participant, individually
Avoid “Did you do it?” Open with objective care: “I noticed Alex has seemed isolated and down lately. Have you noticed that?” Once the student acknowledges the target’s state, secure a personal commitment: “What’s one small, realistic thing you can do this week to help Alex feel less isolated?”
2
Target Support & Action Monitoring
ongoing · 1-on-1 with target + brief follow-ups with group
Check in with the targeted student to confirm safety and whether peer commitments are actually materializing, alongside brief follow-ups with each group member on their commitment.
3
The Group Summit
once commitments have taken root · full group session
The group meets to formally establish shared responsibility for an inclusive environment. At EMS, this is the existing Trailback Panel process (Wednesdays during PASS) — no new infrastructure needed.
Sequencing — a facilitator decision, not yet standardized

Pikas’s original sequence opens Stage 1 with the presumed ringleader — once the most influential member commits, the rest tend to follow. Starting with the most socially secure student instead trades a harder first conversation for an easier momentum-builder. Both are documented practice (Rigby) — worth EMS picking one and standardizing across Trailback Panel facilitators rather than deciding case by case.
